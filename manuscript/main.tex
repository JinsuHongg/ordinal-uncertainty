\title{Classifier-Head Contributions to Rare-End Inward Localization in Imbalanced Ordinal Classification}
\author{Anonymous Authors}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Rare upper ordinal endpoints can be difficult to localize under class
imbalance, but aggregate accuracy and ordinal error do not reveal whether their
predictions are displaced toward interior classes. We study this pattern as
rare-end inward localization and analyze its representation and
classifier-head components. Using routing, predictive location, and inward
shrinkage diagnostics, we identify systematic inward localization in the
studied imbalanced ordinal settings. We then use a fixed-norm, fixed-bias
direction-only classifier-head intervention as a controlled probe. Across
RetinaMNIST and Solar under CE- and RPS-trained representations, the
intervention reduces rare-end localization error, with the effect sign
replicated across all four confirmatory backbone seeds per setting.
Endpoint-specific representation geometry provides additional explanatory value
in Solar, but is partial in RetinaMNIST CE and unsupported under the
preregistered criterion in RetinaMNIST RPS. In controlled RetinaMNIST studies,
balanced sampling is the dominant observed adaptation signal, whereas reduced
rare-class support does not yield a clean monotonic localization dose response.
These results characterize a reproducible head-actionable component of
rare-end localization in the studied settings while bounding the role of
centroid geometry and avoiding a claim of a universal classifier correction.
\end{abstract}
