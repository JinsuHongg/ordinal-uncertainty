\subsection{Rare-end inward localization}
\label{sec:results-phenomenon}

In the studied imbalanced ordinal settings, rare upper extremes exhibit
systematic inward localization. This pattern is distinct from generic
misclassification because the predicted ordinal location is displaced toward
interior classes. Under the original CE classifiers, upper-end routing, the
predictive mean, and inward shrinkage all indicate an interior displacement in
RetinaMNIST and Solar. The severity differs by dataset and is not compared as a
common scale. Figure~\ref{fig:phenomenon} summarizes the phenomenon using
rare-end routing, predictive location, and lower-versus-upper endpoint MAE.

\subsection{Representation and classifier-head decomposition}
\label{sec:results-decomposition}

Historical seed-0 analyses provide a descriptive decomposition of this
failure. Some true rare-end samples are representation-inward under the
training-centroid diagnostic, whereas others retain rare-end-like geometry but
are mapped inward by the original classifier head. The latter group motivates a
head-level intervention because its localization is not fixed by the original
head output. Centroid routing is a diagnostic, not causal proof of
representation collapse or irrecoverability. These historical analyses are
mechanism-forming evidence and are not part of the multi-seed confirmatory
block.

\subsection{Direction-only adaptation: historical evidence}
\label{sec:results-historical-direction}

The original A/C comparisons treated the classifier head as a controlled
intervention. Condition A uses the original frozen head. Condition C preserves
the frozen representation, original class-specific row norms, and original
biases while adapting classifier direction under balanced replacement
sampling. In the historical seed-0 RetinaMNIST and Solar analyses, C moved
rare-end predictions outward, reduced rare-end MAE, and increased exact
rare-end decisions relative to A. These results motivated the confirmatory
backbone-seed protocol below. They are historical/hypothesis-forming results,
not observations included in the confirmatory seed summaries.

\subsection{Confirmatory direction-only replication across backbone seeds}
\label{sec:results-h1}

We next evaluated the frozen H1 criterion on new backbone seeds 1--4 in each
dataset-objective setting. The primary paired endpoint was
$\Delta\mathrm{MAE}=\mathrm{MAE}_{C}-\mathrm{MAE}_{A}$ for rare-end exact-L1
decisions; negative values indicate improved localization. The backbone seed,
not an OOF fold or an individual sample, is the replication unit.

Across all four dataset-objective settings, the direction of the effect
replicated in all four confirmatory backbone seeds
(Table~\ref{tab:confirmatory-h1}). The mean $\Delta\mathrm{MAE}$ was negative
for RetinaMNIST CE and RPS and for Solar CE and RPS. The frozen H1 verdict is
therefore replicated in every setting. The 95\% intervals are descriptive
rather than additional decision gates. In particular, the RetinaMNIST CE
interval crosses zero, but the setting meets the preregistered four-of-four
negative-sign criterion. These results support a reproducible direction-only
localization response under the tested frozen protocols; they do not establish
objective equivalence, a universal classifier correction, or global/UQ
improvement.

\subsection{Endpoint-specific geometry as a setting-dependent diagnostic}
\label{sec:results-h2a}

H2a asks whether endpoint-specific representation geometry adds information
about final C inward shrinkage beyond original-head state and generic centroid
separation. The primary comparison uses $M_0$, which includes the original-head
state and generic separation, and $M_1$, which additionally includes the
endpoint-versus-adjacent representation margin. The outcome is final C inward
shrinkage. H2b is secondary and does not alter this analysis.

The H2a result is setting-dependent (Table~\ref{tab:confirmatory-h2a}). Solar
CE and Solar RPS meet the strong preregistered criterion. RetinaMNIST CE has a
beneficial coefficient sign in all four seeds, but positive incremental
leave-one-out value in only two of four, yielding a partial verdict.
RetinaMNIST RPS has the beneficial coefficient sign in two of four seeds and
does not support H2a. Endpoint-specific geometry therefore provides additional
explanatory value in some settings, rather than a universal explanation for
direction-only recovery.

\subsection{Controlled adaptation-factor study}
\label{sec:results-factorial}

Within the controlled RetinaMNIST study on one frozen RPS representation,
balanced sampling was the dominant observed adaptation factor. Balanced CE and
balanced RPS heads both improved class-4 MAE and the class-4-versus-class-3
margin relative to their natural-sampling counterparts. Differences between CE
and RPS adaptation objectives within a sampling regime were smaller. This
result identifies an associated training signal in this frozen, one-backbone
factorial study. It does not establish that balanced sampling universally drives
direction change or define a new classifier method.

A subsequent RetinaMNIST-only descriptive follow-up repeated the natural-
versus-balanced contrast over CE and RPS backbone seeds 1--4. Natural-sampling
direction adaptation improved endpoint MAE in only two of four seeds for each
objective and was worse than balanced C in every seed. Thus the reproducible
Retina response is specifically a balanced direction-only response; this does
not alter the frozen H1/H2a criteria or make a cross-dataset sampling claim.

\subsection{Rare-support severity study}
\label{sec:results-severity}

The separate full-CE RetinaMNIST support-severity grid provides a boundary on
the imbalance interpretation. Lower class-4 support consistently reduced direct
class-4 evidence, including mean $p_4$ and the class-4-versus-class-3 margin.
However, class-4 MAE, inward shrinkage, severe-error prevalence, and
centroid-routing outcomes were seed-variable and non-monotonic. Support
reduction alone therefore did not produce a clean localization dose-response
explanation. These full-model results are not numerically combined with the
historical frozen-head RetinaMNIST baselines because their protocols and
evaluation populations differ.
