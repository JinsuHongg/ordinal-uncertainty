\section{Introduction}
Rare endpoint errors in ordinal classification have direction: an adjacent error differs from a prediction displaced several classes toward the interior. We call this pattern \emph{rare-end inward localization}. Routing, predictive location, and inward shrinkage expose it, whereas aggregate accuracy and MAE alone do not. We describe it in the studied imbalanced settings without identifying imbalance as an isolated cause.

This distinction is consequential for rare upper endpoints. A classifier can have low endpoint recall because it makes mostly adjacent mistakes, or because its predictive mass and decisions are systematically displaced toward central classes. Those cases imply different ordinal decision burdens, yet are often collapsed into one minority-class accuracy summary. The observed directional pattern motivates a decomposition: some samples may already be inward in a learned feature geometry, while others can retain endpoint-like geometry but be mapped inward by the classifier head.

Long-tail work separates representation and classifier-head effects through decoupled fitting and rebalancing \citep{kang2020decoupling,alshammari2022weight,menon2021logit}; geometry can also matter under imbalance \citep{c2am2022angular,zhu2022balanced,yi2025geometry,wang2026alignment}. Ordinal methods account for label order but need not identify error direction \citep{zhu2019smor,lazaro2023bayesian}. We use these ideas as controls, not as a new classifier method. Our question is narrow: with a representation frozen, can constrained classifier-direction change alter rare-end localization, and when does an endpoint-specific geometry diagnostic help explain final localization?

We address this question as an empirical mechanism analysis. We compare the original frozen head (A) to a balanced, direction-only adaptation (C) that fixes the representation, each class-row norm, and each bias. RetinaMNIST and Solar, each with CE- and RPS-trained backbones, provide the confirmatory block through independently trained seeds. A separate centroid diagnostic tests whether endpoint-specific geometry explains final post-adaptation localization; it is intentionally evaluated as a diagnostic rather than presumed to be a causal account.

\textbf{Contributions.} We characterize rare-end inward localization; show a replicated direction-only response in RetinaMNIST and Solar under CE/RPS; and bound the interpretation: geometry is setting-dependent, and controlled sampling/support studies do not establish universal rebalancing or imbalance causality.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Classifier-side long-tail adaptation.} Decoupled representation/classifier training, weight balancing, and logit adjustment provide established ways to alter tail decisions without treating the representation as the only intervention point \citep{kang2020decoupling,alshammari2022weight,menon2021logit}. They establish important controls for interpreting a rare-class gain, but balanced classifier retraining or direction change is not itself this paper's contribution. We instead use a constrained head adaptation to ask where a rare ordinal endpoint moves in the label space.

\paragraph{Geometry and alignment under imbalance.} Long-tail work has studied class geometry, contrastive feature structure, classifier norms and directions, and feature-classifier alignment \citep{c2am2022angular,zhu2022balanced,yi2025geometry,wang2026alignment}. These results motivate testing both head-responsive and representation-diagnostic accounts. Our training-derived centroid analysis is deliberately narrower: it asks whether endpoint-versus-adjacent alignment adds explanatory value beyond original-head state and generic separation, not whether centroid routing proves a representation cause.

\paragraph{Ordinal imbalance.} Ordinal learning methods use ordered losses, thresholds, and imbalance-aware objectives \citep{zhu2019smor,lazaro2023bayesian}. Our contribution is distinct in research question: we characterize rare-end inward localization and use a fixed-norm, fixed-bias direction-only probe to test its classifier-head-responsive component across trained representations, while reporting the setting-dependent limits of a centroid-based explanation. We do not claim that classifier direction, resampling, or representation geometry is newly discovered.

\section{Problem Setup and Protocol}
For ordered labels \(\{0,\ldots,K-1\}\), predictive location is \(\mu_p(x)=\sum_k kp_k(x)\), and inward shrinkage for a true upper endpoint is \(S(x)=(K-1)-\mu_p(x)\). We report routing, predictive location, shrinkage, exact-L1 MAE, and severe errors; all rare-end quantities condition on the true endpoint population. CE is the nominal baseline and RPS is the ordinal probabilistic baseline.

The modal decision is \(\hat y_{\rm mode}=\arg\max_k p_k(x)\). The exact discrete L1 decision is \(\hat y_{L1}=\arg\min_a\sum_kp_k(x)|k-a|\), a posterior median under the saved deterministic tie rule; we also retain the corresponding L2 decision where reported. An error is severe when it skips at least one adjacent ordinal category. These distinctions matter because endpoint probability, predictive mean, and a discrete decision need not change together. A movement in \(p_{K-1}\), for example, does not by itself identify a movement in the L1 action or in the learned representation.

Condition A is the original classifier applied to a learned frozen representation. Condition C starts at A and adapts only unit weight directions: \(w_k^C=s_k^A v_k^C\), preserving every original norm \(s_k^A\) and bias. C uses replacement-based balanced sampling and a common CE adaptation objective. Thus an A--C difference is a head-actionable response for a fixed representation, not representation improvement.

More explicitly, the original logits are \(z_k(x)=w_k^\top h(x)+b_k\), with \(w_k=s_kv_k\), \(s_k=\lVert w_k\rVert_2\), and \(\lVert v_k\rVert_2=1\). In C, \(s_k\) and \(b_k\) are fixed at their A values and only \(v_k\) is trainable. C is therefore a controlled mechanism probe rather than a proposed deployment classifier: it does not isolate every possible head mechanism, but tests whether a direction change alone is sufficient to alter rare-end localization under fixed representation, scale, and bias.

The confirmatory units are independently trained backbone seeds 1--4 for RetinaMNIST and Solar under CE and RPS. RetinaMNIST uses training-only five-fold OOF head evaluation; Solar uses its aligned archived readout. H1 is \(\Delta\mathrm{MAE}=\mathrm{MAE}_C-\mathrm{MAE}_A\) at the rare endpoint, and replicates when all four seeds are negative. H2a compares nested HC3 OLS models for final C shrinkage; \(M_1\) adds the endpoint-versus-adjacent centroid margin to original-head state and generic centroid separation. Strong H2a requires four negative coefficients, at least three positive LOO gains, and positive mean gain.

The endpoint-specific margin is \(m_{\rm adj}(x)=\lVert h(x)-\bar h_{K-2}\rVert_2-\lVert h(x)-\bar h_{K-1}\rVert_2\), so larger values indicate that a rare-end feature is closer to its own training-derived centroid than to the adjacent interior centroid. The generic gap is the difference between the two nearest centroid distances. H2a asks whether \(m_{\rm adj}\) explains final C shrinkage after the original A state and this generic separation are already included. \(\Delta_{\rm LOO}=\mathrm{MSE}_{\rm LOO}(M_0)-\mathrm{MSE}_{\rm LOO}(M_1)\); positive values favor the endpoint-specific term. Standardization is fitted separately within seed and within each LOO training fold. These geometry quantities are descriptive diagnostics, not causal labels for representation failure.

Writing \(Y_C=(K-1)-\mu_p^C\) and \(Y_A=(K-1)-\mu_p^A\), the nested models are \(M_0:Y_C\sim z(Y_A)+z(g)\) and \(M_1:Y_C\sim z(Y_A)+z(g)+z(m_{\rm adj})\). A negative \(\beta_{\rm ord}\) means stronger endpoint alignment is associated with less final inward shrinkage after the specified controls. H2a is strong with four beneficial coefficient signs, at least three positive LOO gains, and positive mean gain; partial support permits a weaker beneficial pattern. This is an explanatory diagnostic layered on the A/C response, not an alternative selection criterion.

Seed 0 and the B/D head probes were inspected during mechanism development and remain historical context only. A backbone seed, rather than an OOF fold or individual example, is the replication unit. This distinction prevents a large number of held-out examples from being interpreted as independent confirmations.

The design also separates prediction from decision. A posterior distribution can retain some endpoint probability while its exact discrete L1 action remains inward; conversely, a decision change need not imply a representation change. We therefore evaluate the original probability output, the exact L1 action, and localization summaries together. The representation is trained before the Retina OOF procedure and is not retrained in each fold. For Solar, centroids are fit only from aligned training features, not from the archived readout. These controls make the head and geometry interpretations more specific, while still leaving them observational within the stated datasets.

In a one-backbone frozen RetinaMNIST factorial, balanced sampling was the dominant observed adaptation signal; within-regime CE/RPS differences were smaller. A later descriptive natural-sampling follow-up across CE/RPS backbone seeds 1--4 reached the same bounded conclusion: N improved endpoint MAE in only two of four seeds per objective and was worse than balanced C in every seed. The Retina response is therefore specifically a balanced direction-only response, not a generic natural-sampling direction effect. A separate full-CE support-severity grid lowered direct class-4 evidence but did not produce a clean monotonic localization response (Figure~\ref{fig:factor-severity}).

In the factorial notation, the balanced cells C/F outperform the natural-sampling cells E/G on the central rare-end readouts (approximately \(C\simeq F<E\simeq G\)). This is a controlled Retina frozen-RPS result, not a novel rebalancing method or a universal sampling claim. Likewise, reduced class-4 support consistently lowered \(p_4\) and the class-4-versus-class-3 margin, while MAE, shrinkage, severe burden, and feature-nearest routing were seed-variable and non-monotonic. The severity study is therefore a partial boundary on the imbalance interpretation, not a clean causal dose response.

\paragraph{Global trade-off.} Using the same confirmatory A/C populations and exact-L1 decisions, C improves endpoint MAE in every seed but increases mean global MAE in Retina CE/RPS by \(+.015\)/\(+.056\) and Solar CE/RPS by \(+.089\)/\(+.062\). Macro MAE nevertheless improves in all four settings, indicating that the cost is not a uniform degradation across true classes. Endpoint probability mass increases for every true class, most strongly for the adjacent and endpoint classes; C therefore redistributes predictions upward rather than providing a cost-free global improvement. This reinforces its use as a mechanism probe rather than a deployment recommendation.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The robust result is the repeated head-level response, not a deployment method. Because C fixes representation, row norms, and biases, it identifies a direction-responsive head component but does not show that direction is the sole source of error or that global accuracy, calibration, risk, or UQ improve. H2a is weaker: centroid geometry is strong in Solar, partial in RetinaMNIST CE, and unsupported in RetinaMNIST RPS. Historical decomposition is descriptive, not a causal classification of samples.

The distinction from prior long-tail work is therefore substantive but bounded. Balanced classifier fitting and head/feature geometry are established tools; this paper neither introduces them nor ranks them as production methods. It uses a constrained direction-only comparison to characterize an ordinal endpoint failure, whose inward displacement is not recoverable from nominal tail accuracy alone. Balanced sampling in the controlled RetinaMNIST factorial is an associated signal in one frozen representation, not a recommended universal recipe.

There are several limits on external interpretation. The two confirmatory populations have different evaluation roles: RetinaMNIST is training-only OOF, whereas Solar is a fixed archived readout rather than a new external cohort per backbone. UTKFace contributes only historical seed-0 support. The endpoint supports and label structures differ, and centroid distances are deliberately coarse. Consequently, neither the replication results nor the controlled support grid establish a dataset-independent law of ordinal imbalance. They instead justify the narrower conclusion that a rare-end localization component is direction-responsive under the frozen comparisons studied here.

The evidence covers two confirmatory datasets and two backbone objectives. Solar uses a fixed archived readout; UTKFace is historical supporting evidence only. Centroid distances are coarse diagnostics, and the controlled factor study uses one frozen representation. The non-monotonic support study does not causally identify imbalance severity. These boundaries motivate a conservative mechanism claim rather than a universal rebalancing result.

\section{Conclusion}
Rare upper endpoints can be localized inward in ways not captured by aggregate ordinal summaries. In the studied settings, fixed-norm, fixed-bias direction-only adaptation reproducibly improves rare-end localization across confirmatory seeds, while geometry and support effects remain setting-specific. The contribution is an empirical mechanism analysis, not a general classifier correction.
