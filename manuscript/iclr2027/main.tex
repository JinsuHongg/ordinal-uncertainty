\title{Rare-Endpoint Error Redistribution under Classifier-Head Adaptation in Imbalanced \mbox{Ordinal Classification}}
\author{Anonymous Authors}
\begin{document}\maketitle
\begin{abstract}Rare ordinal endpoints can be mislocalized toward interior classes, with error distances not determined by aggregate accuracy or ordinal error alone. We characterize this pattern as rare-end inward localization and separate representation and classifier-head contributions with a fixed-norm, fixed-bias, balanced-sampling head probe that updates only class directions. Across RetinaMNIST and Solar with cross-entropy (CE)- and ranked probability score (RPS)-trained frozen representations, the balanced probe improves rare-end localization under the exact L1 decision rule in every confirmatory backbone seed. These gains accompany label-space error redistribution: global mean absolute error (MAE) increases while macro MAE improves in the studied balanced-probe comparisons. Endpoint-specific centroid geometry adds predictive value in Solar, is partial in RetinaMNIST CE, and is not supported in RetinaMNIST RPS. Across settings, natural-sampling direction-only adaptation is weaker and less consistent than the balanced probe, indicating that the strong rare-end response is not explained by direction updates alone. Reduced rare-class support does not yield a monotonic dose response. The results are setting-dependent and do not establish a universal correction.\end{abstract}
