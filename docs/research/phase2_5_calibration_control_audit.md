# Phase 2.5 — Calibration-Control Audit

## Motivation and protocol

Phase 2 found that RPS training improved several decision-risk endpoints relative
to CE, but did not uniformly improve ordinal decisions. This audit tests whether
that risk advantage is explained by scalar probability sharpness. It uses the
already frozen native-28x28 RetinaMNIST CE and RPS models for seeds 0--4. No
models were retrained and no checkpoint was changed.

For each model and seed, validation logits were regenerated by deterministic
inference from the selected frozen checkpoint because validation outputs had not
been persisted. A separate scalar temperature was fitted by minimizing validation
NLL only:

\[
p_k^{(T)} = \operatorname{softmax}(z/T)_k,\qquad T>0.
\]

All calibrated metrics use the untouched official test set. Test labels did not
enter temperature fitting. Test sample identifiers and labels matched exactly
across all CE/RPS seed artifacts (400 samples per seed).

Temperature scaling preserves mode decisions, as expected. It altered CE's L1
decision on 1.45% +/- 1.27% of samples, versus 7.40% +/- 6.28% for RPS. Thus,
it slightly improves CE calibration and severe detection, but does not make the
post-hoc decision geometry identical.

## Risk and class-wise diagnostics

For both models, mean L1 Bayes risk was higher for adjacent and severe errors
than correct predictions, but the adjacent/severe group means overlap; the audit
does not assume strict severity monotonicity. Calibration mostly shifts the scale
of risk rather than creating a new error-severity separation.

Extreme class 4 remains a major limitation under the L1 decision: mean accuracy
was zero for every condition across these runs. Its raw severe-error prevalence
was 76.0% for CE and 82.0% for RPS; after temperature scaling it was 77.0% and
95.0%, respectively. These small extreme-class counts should be interpreted with
care. Class 0 is substantially better (raw L1 accuracy 74.0% CE and 72.4% RPS).

## Interpretation and gate

**Decision: MIXED AFTER CALIBRATION.** Calibration improves NLL/ECE for both
models and narrows CE's severe-detection gap, but temperature-scaled RPS retains
stronger L1-risk alignment (0.4224 vs 0.3829), severe AUROC (0.6517 vs 0.6192),
severe AUPRC (0.2940 vs 0.2699), and lower mean ordinal-MAE selective risk
(0.4479 vs 0.4825). Conversely, calibrated RPS loses its raw L1 MAE advantage
and requires more variable mode-to-L1 correction.

The Phase 3 gate is **JUSTIFIED**, but only for the specific unresolved gap:
existing RPS training retains useful calibrated ordinal risk geometry without
producing consistent gains in ordinal decisions, severe-error burden, or the
extreme class. This audit does not define a Phase 3 loss or method.
